How to read these numbers

  1. Which articles? The archive contains 5,000 Global Voices articles. In its licensed English-language development set, 248 articles contained Muslim, Muslims, Islamophobia or Islamophobic in the extracted body after holdout exclusions. A fixed hash order selected ten per year, with distinct story groups and content hashes. Selection used the text before opening model results. This search can include a personal name or miss an article that uses other terms. Equal numbers per year give each year the same space. They do not measure how often the topic appeared in the news.
  2. What was checked? Six frozen criteria applied to 137 source units in 60 full extracted articles. The original Gemma model run retained 822 decisions here, including 49 flags, 11 uncertain, 525 criterion unmet and 237 outside the criterion. 0 selected decisions are missing. The wider parent run is partial.
  3. How are articles counted? For each criterion, one or more flags count the article once. Without a flag, missing decisions take precedence, then uncertainty, then a checked unit without a flag, then all units outside the criterion. One article can appear in multiple criterion rows. Original unit-level outcomes, including uncertainty alongside a flag, are available in the workbook.
  4. Who interpreted the text? The original model assigned the outcomes, speaker and stance. Positive and uncertain readings have grounded detail when supplied. For a compact decision, DOXA attached the scored unit’s sentences in software. The six teaching examples were chosen afterwards. Their commentary is AI-authored. The original outputs have no independent human reference labels.

These figures describe one selected publisher and the model’s readings. Estimating how common prejudice is, comparing outlets, or measuring missed and incorrect flags needs another sampling design and independent review.

Where the criteria come from

  • Defining the construct. Different definitions change what a measure can capture. Here each row names the textual criterion and its evidence requirements. Erik Bleich (2011). What Is Islamophobia and How Much Is There?
  • Defining the harm. Specify the harm, the people affected and the interpretation a measure permits. A count of article flags cannot measure a reporter’s private beliefs. Su Lin Blodgett, Solon Barocas, Hal Daumé III and Hanna Wallach (2020). Language (Technology) is Power.
  • Reading stance. Stance needs a specific target. For each reading, ask who supports which proposition. The original tweet benchmark supplies no accuracy estimate for these articles. Saif Mohammad et al. (2016). SemEval-2016 Task 6. Detecting Stance in Tweets.
  • Independent review. Independent annotations and reported disagreement would be needed to assess how consistently people apply the rubric. This study has no independent reference labels. Ron Artstein and Massimo Poesio (2008). Inter-Coder Agreement for Computational Linguistics.
  • Operational framework. The first three criteria adapt England’s non-statutory guidance to textual review. Applying them retrospectively across countries gives a reading under this framework, without deciding legal liability. UK Government (2026). A Definition of Anti-Muslim Hostility.
  • Operational framework. The second framework prompts separate questions about group generalization, exclusion and institutional practice. A claim about repeated practice needs corroboration beyond a single passage. Runnymede Trust (2025). Islamophobia: towards a working definition.
