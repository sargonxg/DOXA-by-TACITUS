Each locator is `source_id / block_id [start:end]`, using zero-based Unicode code points, end exclusive. Quotations below reproduce the complete retained evidence span. The source IDs link to the publications listed below. Fields are actual model output, not auditor corrections.

1. **An anti-Jewish criterion applied to a Muslim target.** Finding `9f711e5c958aae65e57fe74684e0b867`, [uk01], `uk01-b00816[80:144]`: “a “cash cow which any enterprising Muslim group could tap into”.” Nexus `anti_jewish_tropes`, category `anti_jewish_tropes`; speaker `newspaper article`, attribution `reported_speaker`, stance `not_applicable`, certainty `insufficient_context`. The explanation admits the anti-Jewish criterion is being applied “in a different religious context.” Uncertainty does not repair the target mismatch. The alternative, criticism of financial opportunism, deserves examination under an appropriate rubric without treating structural resemblance as anti-Jewish evidence.

2. **General antisemitism routed to the Israel criterion.** Finding `2853eb6abc4a66ff3c664da9129b1729`, [uk01], `uk01-b00043[4:278]`: “I was disturbed by the prevalence of antisemitism within the Channel cases I observed. Individuals discussed at Channel panels tended to harbour violent and fanatical beliefs about Jews, often expressing an intent to kill, assault or blow-up members of the Jewish community.” IHRA `israel_related_context`, category `stance`; speaker `William Shawcross CVO`, attribution `author`, stance `opposes`, certainty `supported`. The explanation acknowledges no explicit Israel link. The anti-Jewish hostility is evidenced, but criterion selection and the unrelated category warrant review.

3. **A supported finding whose explanation says its criterion is untriggered.** Finding `dc911f823c6e9c29c5412beebe626aa9`, [uk05], `uk05-p0134-b0002[1729:2104]`: “We have also heard about cases where devout Orthodox Jewish women in some communities, despite being able to get a divorce in British law courts, have felt trapped in a marriage they cannot get out of, as only the husband has the power to grant a ‘get’ (a Jewish divorce document authenticated by a Rabbi and given by a husband to his wife releasing her from their marriage).” IHRA `israel_related_context`, category `ihra`; speaker `Dame Louise Casey DBE CB`, attribution `author`, stance `not_applicable`, certainty `supported`. Explanation: “there is no mention of Israel or the state of Israel, thus this criterion is not triggered.” This creates ambiguity between supported evidence of a boundary/nonmatch and a supported criterion finding.

4. **Poll attribution loses the respondent group and intent limit.** Finding `d76f261c437e406817752b5c54d4c777`, [uk01], `uk01-b00800[547:704]`: ““people associate Islam with terrorism” (59%), followed by “it means all Muslims are treated as suspects” (53%) and “it is unfair on innocent Muslims” (52%).” Anti-Muslim hostility `stereotyping_and_intent`, same category; speaker `general public (via Crest poll)`, attribution `reported_speaker`, stance `not_applicable`, certainty `supported`. The block introduces British Muslim respondents expressing concerns, then says the general public broadly shared them. The evidence reports objections to stereotyping; it does not establish an actor's intent to encourage hatred. Review both attribution specificity and the required uncertainty.

5. **Quotation control: opposition is directed at the reported target.** Finding `40d8888e3d373b19024d6cc9d55c50c6`, [uk01], `uk01-b00188[169:237]`: “comparing the actions of the Israeli government to that of the Nazis”. IHRA `israel_related_context`, category `ihra`; speaker `unnamed individual`, attribution `reported_speaker`, stance `opposes`, certainty `supported`; target `Israeli government`. Here `opposes` can describe the reported individual's position toward that target. It should not automatically be diagnosed as the author's stance leaking into the quotation. The alternative invokes JDA/Nexus political-expression protections; those require separate contextual judgments.

6. **Hypothetical counter-prejudice control.** Finding `031ba70141ac1cf94a2d12b9c5a5a7a0`, [us10], `us10-p0003-b0005[59:188]`: “Coworkers begin to make antisemitic jokes and comments to him based on stereotypes about Jews and news events surrounding Israel.” Nexus `anti_jewish_tropes`, same category; speaker `Coworkers (hypothetical)`, attribution `reported_speaker`, stance `opposes`, certainty `supported`; target `Jews`. Explanation and alternative identify a hypothetical discrimination example. The model preserves that status; the finding must not become a claim that DOJ endorses or observed these particular events. Group targets still make stance less precise than an explicit proposition would.

7. **Uncertainty preserved, but the explanation strengthens the scenario.** Finding `0da3c1f36491dd937b786b3ed04d84cc`, [us06], `us06-b00019[3:283]`: “John is Muslim. In the last few months, a coworker regularly seeks him out for long discussions about Islam, ISIS, and terrorism. In a routine check-in with his supervisor, John tells the supervisor about these discussions and says that he is increasingly uncomfortable with them.” Anti-Muslim hostility `stereotyping_and_intent`, same category; speaker `unnamed coworker (reported speaker)`, attribution `reported_speaker`, stance `supports`, certainty `insufficient_context`; target `John (Muslim)`. Intent uncertainty is appropriate. “repeatedly forcing discussions” in the explanation exceeds “regularly seeks him out”; `supports` lacks a specified proposition and is difficult to interpret.

8. **A political-expression alternative abstracts away the actual treatment.** Finding `f663038767f79e832a2fbdeeb0f3305e`, [us01], `us01-p0040-b0006[0:188]`: “On college campuses, Jewish students, educators, and administrators have been derided, ostracized, and sometimes discriminated against because of their actual or perceived views on Israel.” JDA `political_expression_boundary`, category `framing`; speaker `White House, Biden administration`, attribution `author`, stance `supports`, certainty `supported`. The alternative suggests “such treatment” might be political criticism. The same block alleges denial of participation credit to Jewish students and a Kristallnacht reference. Review whether the alternative addresses these actions, and what proposition `supports` denotes; protection for political criticism does not settle discrimination allegations.

## Reproduction and limits

All 15 evidence strings were checked against `documents[].blocks[].text[start:end]`. Each exported finding was also matched to its persisted SQLite finding/task and `data/benchmark-cloud-v1.2/objects/runs/<run_id>/tasks/<task_id>.json` using the locator, quote, lens, criterion, category, speaker, attribution, stance, certainty, explanation and alternative. Task objects omit public finding IDs; join SQLite `findings.task_id` to `tasks.id` to recover `result_key`. Some retained findings belong to tasks marked `failed` because their other windows failed: retention is not proof of whole-task completion.

Taxonomy fragmentation is directly visible within this sample: categories include `stance` for an IHRA criterion, `framing` for JDA, plain `ihra`, and both bare and lens-prefixed criterion names. Category-only aggregation could merge different frameworks or split comparable observations; inspect `lens_id` and `criterion` together. This report records the behavior without changing the engine or declaring every debatable interpretation a defect.

Selection deliberately sought difficult quotation, counter-prejudice, attribution, boundary and explanation cases. It is not random, exhaustive, independently adjudicated, or a measure of model prevalence. Evidence-slice equality establishes textual traceability only. No new inference was run, and no external historical, legal, statistical or allegation-level fact-check was performed. Layout, images and unprocessed source text remain outside this audit; absent findings cannot establish absence of a discourse pattern. Review should preserve framework differences and avoid inferring authors' identities, private beliefs or intentions.
