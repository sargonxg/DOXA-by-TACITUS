How to read this guide.

Begin with the exact source wording. Check the represented speaker, criterion and proposition. Read the recorded model interpretation, then use the distinctly labeled walkthrough note to question its limits.

Keep the voices apart.

A document may quote, report, reject or invent a speaker for an example. The represented speaker’s stance and the document’s stance are separate assessments.

Recorded model interpretation

The text frames the conflict as a legal obligation under Title VII, where customer preference or brand image does not constitute undue hardship, necessitating religious accommodation.

Recorded alternative: The text could frame this as a conflict between corporate branding and civil rights.

AI-authored walkthrough commentary · separate from the model record

The recorded frame prioritizes religious accommodation over a client's preferred image or dress code. This is a descriptive observation about protective guidance, not an indicator that the EEOC endorses the discriminatory preference.

The passage mixes an institutional explanation of a rule with an example character, Jon. Preserve that distinction: Jon is not evidence of a real incident. The record's hypothetical=false flag needs contextual review, since a general rule and its fictional illustration can coexist in one passage.

The source contains historical guidance and a notice about Groff v. DeJoy. Use the frozen wording to study its framing; check current authoritative law separately before applying its legal explanation. Neither the protected group nor an author's private beliefs can be inferred from a raw count of religious terms.

Review question: Who expresses the customer's preference, who states the protective rule, and which parts belong to an illustrative scenario rather than a reported event?

Keep three levels apart: the Government Response, the committee recommendation reproduced within it, and the people whose experiences the committee describes. Recorded model interpretation

The text reports an individual's stance that pro-Palestinian organizations should work to end Zionism without fearing to hurt Zionist sensibilities, which falls under political expression regarding Zionism.

Recorded alternative: The individual's advocacy for ending Zionism is presented as a political position rather than inherent prejudice.

AI-authored walkthrough commentary · separate from the model record

The model applies the Jerusalem Declaration's political-expression boundary to a reported statement about ending Zionism. A boundary being met is a limit on classification; it is not another positive prejudice count.

The statement is attributed to an individual within a government review. Do not merge the individual's position with the review author's position. Read the surrounding material to identify what proposition each speaker is supporting, reporting or opposing.

This result does not declare every other statement in the surrounding discussion harmless or settle a person's intentions. Other passages and other frameworks may produce different assessments. Keep IHRA, Jerusalem Declaration and Nexus separate rather than voting them into one universal score.

Review question: Which exact political proposition falls within the stated boundary, and is any separate expression directed at people as people being assessed under its own criterion?

Recorded model interpretation

While the text describes hostile acts, it does not provide evidence of specific stereotyping intended to encourage hatred, nor does it establish the perpetrator's intent. Context is insufficient to meet this criterion.

Recorded alternative: The text lacks details on stereotyping or intent.

AI-authored walkthrough commentary · separate from the model record

The record remains uncertain under a criterion requiring stereotyping and intent. Testimony about hostile treatment can establish what a speaker reports experiencing without, by itself, establishing a perpetrator's specific intent to encourage hatred.

The quoted person describes being targeted. Keep that testimony separate from the containing institution's explanation and from any inference about the people responsible. The walkthrough does not convert the speaker's account into either a verified legal finding or a dismissed complaint.

Uncertainty is specific to this criterion and supplied context. It does not imply that no harm occurred or that another criterion cannot be met. The appropriate next step is to inspect surrounding evidence and declare what additional information would resolve the question.

Review question: Which requirement is unresolved—target, conduct, stereotyping or intent—and what evidence would address that requirement without forcing a positive or negative verdict?
